Calculation Model & Glossary

This page explains how to read the WFM Priority Engine, how KPI performance is weighted, how Revenue-based Hot List/Pareto ranking works, how operational KPI overlap is controlled, how statuses work, and how to interpret the What If simulator. It is a reading guide — not an update procedure.

Model V7.2 Source: Raw KPIs JH Entity grain: Country + Client + Month Revenue materiality: MLOTT Denominador ABS priority metric: ABS Ácido only MLOTT + ABS Corregido excluded from priority

How to Read the Engine

Recommended sequence for an executive review.

1

Size the issue

Start in Executive Dashboard. Read status distribution, Top 10 Adverse Revenue Share and the Hot List. Only accounts with at least one adverse risk-eligible KPI enter the Hot List; they are then ranked by Revenue materiality in the selected scope.

2

Find concentration

Move to Portfolio Intelligence. Use the Revenue Pareto, primary-driver mix and portfolio breakdown to identify where management attention is concentrated.

3

Validate the driver

Use Trends & Drivers to confirm whether the KPI miss is structural or temporary. Historical KPI evolution uses the KPI's own denominator, not a simple account average.

4

Test the action

Use What If to estimate the directional B2P response to a change in ABS Ácido, ATT or SHKG. Treat the result as scenario support, not a committed forecast.

Calculation Flow

From source KPI performance to executive Hot List and Pareto.

Step 1
Weighted Actual & Target
Native KPI denominator is used whenever available.
Step 2
Adverse Gap
Only the unfavorable side of Actual vs Target enters prioritization.
Step 3
ΔPPH = Gap × PPH
Operational-hours equivalent used to size the deviation.
Step 4
Revenue Materiality
MLOTT Denominador is used only as the current account revenue weight.
Step 5
Adverse Eligibility
An account becomes rank-eligible when at least one risk KPI is adverse versus target.
Step 6
Operational Driver
ΔPPH and correlation help identify the dominant KPI issue; they do not change Revenue rank.
Step 7
Revenue Rank, Pareto & Status
Adverse accounts are ordered by Revenue; Pareto is cumulative adverse-account Revenue share.

Core Formulas

Exact business logic presented by the report.

Weighted performance
Actual = Σ Actual Numerator ÷ Σ Actual Denominator
Target = Σ Target Numerator ÷ Σ Target Denominator
If a numerator/pond is absent, the engine reconstructs it as rate × denominator.
Adverse KPI gap
Lower-is-better: max(Actual − Target, 0)
Higher-is-better: max(Target − Actual, 0)
Only unfavorable deviation contributes to the priority signal.
Operational translation
ΔPPH = Adverse Rate × PPH
Makes KPI misses comparable as an operational-hours equivalent.
Revenue-based account rank
Rank Value = Revenue, only if the account has ≥1 adverse risk KPI
Otherwise Rank Value = 0
KPI severity determines eligibility and action; Revenue determines the order of eligible accounts.
Revenue Index & Share
Revenue Index = Account Revenue ÷ Highest Revenue among adverse accounts × 100
Revenue Share = Account Revenue ÷ Total Revenue of adverse accounts
Pareto and Hot List use these relative shares; raw Revenue amounts are not displayed.

Overlap Control

Used to interpret operational KPI drivers without allowing duplicated KPI signals to distort the driver narrative.

1 · Eligible KPI set
ABS Ácido only

ABS Corregido is excluded from the priority engine, so absence is represented only by ABS Ácido in Hot List, Pareto and status classification.

2 · Statistical overlap
r ≥ 0.60 · n ≥ 30

Pearson correlation is calculated on adverse KPI gaps across client-month observations. Positive overlap is considered only when both the correlation and minimum observation thresholds are met. Maximum adjustment is 75%.

3 · Guardrail
Largest driver preserved

The operational driver signal cannot fall below the largest individual adverse driver. Revenue rank and Pareto are not reduced by correlation.

Correlation rule: adjustment starts at 0% when r = 0.60 and scales progressively to a maximum 75% at r = 1.00 for operational driver sizing only. It does not change Revenue rank or Pareto. Correlation is an overlap indicator, not proof that one KPI causes another.

Risk Status & Pareto Rules

Statuses are recalculated inside the currently selected portfolio and period.

Critical Review
#1 Revenue-ranked adverse account, any adverse account with ≥10% of adverse-account Revenue, or accounts encountered before cumulative adverse Revenue reaches 55%.
Continuous Review
Remaining Revenue-ranked adverse accounts encountered before cumulative adverse Revenue reaches 85%.
Recovery Watch
Residual adverse account beyond the main Revenue concentration zone, with no worsening driver and at least one improving driver.
Watchlist
Residual adverse account outside the main concentration or an adverse account lacking valid Revenue for ranking.
Healthy
No adverse risk-eligible KPI deviation in the selected period. Pareto applies only to adverse accounts and follows cumulative Revenue share up to 80%.

Glossary

Core terms used across the report.

Adverse Gap
Only the unfavorable portion of Actual versus Target after considering whether higher or lower is better.
ΔPPH
Productive-paid-hours equivalent associated with the adverse KPI deviation.
Revenue Materiality
Account Revenue from MLOTT Denominador used to rank adverse accounts and build Pareto concentration; raw Revenue is not displayed.
Revenue Index
Relative 0–100 index versus the highest-Revenue adverse account in the current scope.
Revenue Share
Account Revenue divided by total Revenue across rank-eligible adverse accounts.
Pareto
Revenue-ranked adverse accounts flagged while cumulative adverse-account Revenue before the account remains below 80%.
Primary Driver
Largest operational adverse KPI driver based on ΔPPH after overlap controls; it does not determine Revenue rank.
Worsening / Improving
Direction from the linear trend of the favorable Actual-vs-Target gap over up to the latest 3 available periods.
Correlation r
Pearson coefficient from −1 to +1. Only qualifying positive adverse-gap correlation can reduce overlapping priority signal.
Top N
Hot List display control. It changes how many ranked accounts are shown; it does not change the underlying portfolio calculation.
Native Denominator
KPI-specific denominator used to aggregate Actual and Target correctly instead of taking a simple average across accounts.
Revenue Coverage
Share of adverse accounts with valid Revenue materiality data. Accounts without valid Revenue remain visible but cannot receive a Revenue rank.
Scenario B2P
Modeled B2P after changing one What If driver while maintaining the source Actual/Target values untouched.
Historical Sensitivity
PPH-weighted historical KPI↔B2P regression used to calibrate scenario magnitude; it does not establish causation.
Confidence
Scenario quality label based on comparable history and consistency of the historical KPI↔B2P relationship.

What If Method

How the B2P scenario should be interpreted.

1 · Drivers
ABS A · ATT · SHKG

Only these operational KPIs can be changed in the simulator. Their lower-is-better direction is enforced so an improvement cannot be modeled as a deterioration.

2 · Historical calibration
Up to 18 months

The selected Client/Country history is used to estimate KPI↔B2P sensitivity. Comparable history and correlation strength determine the confidence level.

3 · Mathematical consistency
BH = B2P × PPH

The scenario keeps B2P, BH and PPH mathematically consistent. It changes scenario assumptions only; source Actuals and Targets are never overwritten.

Interpretation boundary: Hot List and Pareto answer a materiality question: among accounts with adverse KPI performance, which ones carry the most Revenue? KPI gaps and ΔPPH explain the operational issue and action. Revenue is used only for relative ranking and Pareto concentration. What If is directional decision support and does not establish causation or replace an operational forecast.
